Resolve every PERSON on a credit line against an artifact that ENUMERATES people — a paper's author
block, a staff page, a repo's CODEOWNERS — never against a parenthetical in prose that reads like a
nickname. Two people who share a surname fuse into one and the result looks deliberate.

**Why:** a deck credited "Rosen Ting-Ying (Tony) Yu" as a mentor for three review rounds. They are
two people: Rosen Ting-Ying Yu, an MIT co-author on the paper, and Prof. Tony Yu, her your-university
associate mentor. The fused name was never wrong-looking — a middle name plus an English nickname is
the single most ordinary shape a name takes — so no reviewer, mine or Codex's, questioned it. The
author block had the answer the whole time and nobody read it as a LIST OF PEOPLE.

**How to apply:** when a name carries a parenthetical, an alias or a second surname, treat it as TWO
candidate people until an enumerating artifact says otherwise, and check the roles separately: a
co-author list settles authorship and says nothing about mentorship. Expect the roles to come from
different sources (a paper names authors; a fellowship names mentors), so a single artifact that
settles one is not evidence about the other. Where no artifact lists the person at all, write what
the user said and record in the lane that it is unconfirmed — do not infer a title from an
affiliation. Pairs with [[use-the-products-own-label-never-an-invented-name]].
