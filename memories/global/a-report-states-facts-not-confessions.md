A report, page or artifact for the user states **what is true**, not what I previously said that was
not. When a published claim turns out wrong, the repair is to **replace it with the correct
content**, silently — not to add an entry titled "I called five review rounds a waste without
opening them" or "I told you it was waiting on you". Her words: *"You don't need to say that you
made a mistake on the page. Show the correct content."*

**Why:** a confession entry costs the reader twice — they read the wrong version, then the
retraction, and get the fact last, buried under my self-assessment. It also inflates my errors into
the subject of a document that is supposed to be about her business. Meta-commentary about my own
reliability is not information she can act on; the corrected number is.

**How to apply:** at write time, ask of every item — *is the subject of this sentence the project,
or me?* Items about the project (a spec sat unmergeable two days; the provider file has no email
column) stay, phrased as facts with their cost. Items whose subject is my reporting get **deleted**
and their true content folded into the section where it belongs. Never a "What I got wrong"
heading, never "an earlier version of this page said…". This does NOT relax
[[counting-a-set-is-not-classifying-it]] or [[verify-claims-against-artifacts]] — the measuring
still happens, and a wrong claim still gets fixed in place at the same URL; what changes is that
the fix reads as the fact, not as an apology. Say the correction to her **in conversation** if it
changes a decision she has already made; the artifact just carries the truth.
